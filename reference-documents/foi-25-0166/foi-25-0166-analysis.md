# FOI 25-0166: Practical Refusal and Document Classification Contradiction

**Request Date:** 1 April 2025  
**Decision Date:** 3 June 2025  
**Outcome:** Practical refusal under s24(1)(b) FOI Act 1982

---

## Summary

FOI request 25-0166 sought implementation documentation for the TGA COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan across five Plan objectives. The request underwent two rounds of consultation, with scope progressively refined from an estimated 399 documents (3,926 pages) to 30 documents across 12 defined categories. Despite this 92% reduction in scope and explicit TGA guidance that refined items "may be manageable," TGA refused the final request under practical refusal provisions, claiming that classifying documents by Plan objectives required "subjective interpretation"—nine months after successfully classifying 2,218+ pages by identical Plan objectives for the OAIC (September 2024, MR22/00538).

## Request Scope

### Original Request (1 April 2025)

**Item 1:** Implementation documentation for the COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan (February 2021 to April 2025), including:

**(a)** Records of "ample documentation demonstrating compliance" referenced by Ms. Elspeth Kay, Assistant Secretary, Pharmacovigilance Branch (FOI decision 27 February 2022, Ref: D22-5201218)

**(b)** Documents containing specific outputs:
- Criteria for COVID-19 vaccine report escalation and clinical review (page 7 of Plan)
- Specific protocols for investigating COVID-19 AEFI reports (page 8 of Plan)
- Refined processes and statistical methods for analysing observed COVID-19 AEFI rates (page 8 of Plan)

**(c)** Records showing implementation of specific commitments:
- Establishment of standing COVID-19 expert advisory committee (page 9 of Plan)
- Processes to enable rapid action in response to emerging safety concerns (page 10 of Plan)

**Item 2:** Briefings, reports, dashboards, or summaries prepared for TGA senior management, Department Secretary, or Minister documenting or evaluating Plan implementation or effectiveness (February 2021 to April 2025)

**Initial TGA estimate:** 399+ documents (3,926 pages), 180 hours processing time, 10+ third parties requiring consultation.

### Five Plan Objectives Referenced

1. Report collection
2. Signal detection and investigation
3. Action on concerns
4. Public communications
5. Stakeholder collaboration

### Requested Document Categories (20 May 2025 - Final Refinement)

Following two rounds of consultation, request refined to 12 document categories:

1. Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) or equivalents
2. Case processing instructions/guidelines
3. Coding/process updates
4. Workflow process documentation
5. Safety signal investigation protocols
6. Regulatory action processes
7. Public COVID-19 safety communications procedures
8. Stakeholder collaboration documentation
9. Official procedure documentation
10. Internal policy/guidance
11. Implementation guidelines
12. Operational protocols

### Final Refined Scope (20 May 2025)

Six most recent documents per category for each of the five Plan objectives, covering the period February 2021 to February 2025. **Total requested: 30 documents** (92% reduction from initial 399 documents).

Fallback provision: Other TGA-classified documents if fewer than six available per category/objective. The reference to "TGA-classified" documents implies TGA maintains classification systems that could identify documents by category and objective.

## TGA's Refusal Rationale

### Stated Grounds

TGA refused the refined request (Item 2) on grounds that:

1. Classifying documents by Plan objectives required "subjective interpretation"
2. Linking documents to specific Plan objectives involved judgment about "most significant" relevance
3. The task was impracticable despite:
   - Scope refinement from 399 documents to 30 documents (92% reduction)
   - Explicit definition of 12 document categories
   - TGA's prior indication that Items 1(b) and 1(c) "may be manageable"

### Practical Refusal Provisions Applied

Section 24(1)(b) of the FOI Act 1982: Request would substantially and unreasonably divert agency resources.

### Section 24AA(2) Requirements

Section 24AA(2) of the FOI Act requires agencies to assess whether a request would substantially and unreasonably divert resources without regard to the reasons for making the request or the applicant's characteristics.

However, TGA's prior demonstrated capability to classify 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives for OAIC review (September 2024) raises questions about whether classifying 30 documents across 12 predefined categories constitutes an "unreasonable" diversion of resources, particularly when:
- The classification methodology had been successfully applied nine months earlier
- Applicant achieved 92% scope reduction in response to TGA's consultation suggestions
- TGA had indicated refined items "may be manageable" in first consultation

## Observations

### Demonstrated Classification Capability

TGA's position on capability to classify documents by Plan objectives changed between September 2024 and June 2025:

**September 2024 (OAIC MR22/00538):**
- Successfully organised and classified 2,218+ pages of vaccine safety documentation
- Searched 531+ TRIM containers systematically
- Classified material by Plan objectives for Information Commissioner review
- Demonstrated technical and methodological capacity for objective-based classification

**Source:** Therapeutic Goods Administration. (2024). *Response to written notice and direction under ss 55V(2) and 55(2)(e)(ii) of the Freedom of Information Act 1982*. 20 September 2024. OAIC Reference MR22/00538, pp. 28-32.

**June 2025 (FOI 25-0166):**
- Identified initial scope of approximately 399 documents (3,926 pages)
- Applicant refined scope to 30 documents (92% reduction) in response to TGA consultation
- TGA characterised classification by Plan objectives as requiring "subjective interpretation"
- Refused to process despite:
  - Massive scope reduction
  - TGA's own guidance that refined items "may be manageable"
  - Explicit definition of 12 document categories
  - Temporal scope limitation
- Applied practical refusal provisions nine months after demonstrating identical classification capability

### Contradiction in Institutional Position

The refusal demonstrates contradictory institutional positions:

1. **Capability demonstrated:** TGA organised 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives when directed by OAIC (September 2024)

2. **Capability initially acknowledged:** TGA indicated Items 1(b) and 1(c) "may be manageable" in first consultation (24 April 2025)

3. **Capability denied:** TGA characterised organising 30 documents by Plan objectives as impracticably subjective (June 2025) despite 92% scope reduction

4. **Scope differential:** OAIC-directed search covered 531+ containers and 2,218+ pages; refined FOI request sought 30 documents across 12 predefined categories

The practical refusal occurred despite:
- Applicant conducting two rounds of scope refinement in response to TGA guidance
- Request narrowed from ~399 documents (3,926 pages) to 30 documents (92% reduction)
- Document categories explicitly defined (12 categories)
- Temporal scope limited (February 2021-February 2025)
- Fallback provisions for incomplete categories included
- TGA's initial indication that refined scope "may be manageable"

### Scope Reduction Analysis

The progression demonstrates extraordinary applicant responsiveness to TGA consultation:

**Original request (1 April 2025):**
- 399+ documents estimated
- 3,926 pages
- 180 hours processing time

**First refinement (5 May 2025):**
- Narrowed to Items 1-3
- ~45 documents total
- Reduced timeframe

**Second refinement (9 May 2025):**
- 5 most significant documents per objective
- 25 documents total
- Withdrew Item 3

**Final refinement (20 May 2025):**
- 6 most recent documents by category per objective
- **30 documents total**
- **92% reduction from original estimate**
- 12 explicitly defined categories

Despite this 92% scope reduction and TGA's initial guidance that refined items "may be manageable," TGA refused on grounds of subjectivity—nine months after classifying 2,218+ pages by identical Plan objectives for OAIC.

### Implications for Document Organisation

TGA's refusal on subjectivity grounds raises questions about:

1. **Records management:** Whether TGA's document management systems organise material by Plan objectives or activity type

2. **Institutional memory:** Whether TGA maintains systematic implementation records that could be retrieved by objective/output

3. **Verification capability:** Whether TGA can demonstrate Plan implementation if classification by Plan objectives is characterised as subjective

4. **Differential treatment:** Whether classification capability is applied differently for oversight bodies versus citizens

5. **Consultation good faith:** Whether TGA's initial indication that refined scope "may be manageable" reflected genuine assessment or placeholder response

## Relationship to Search Methodology Issues

This practical refusal is distinct from, but related to, search methodology inadequacy documented in OAIC MR22/00538:

**Search methodology issue (MR22/00538):**
- What search terms TGA employed in TRIM searches
- Whether search strategy was adequate to locate implementation evidence
- TGA used 8 generic document-title terms, missing output-specific terminology

**Classification refusal issue (FOI 25-0166):**
- Whether TGA will organise identified documents by Plan objectives
- Whether such classification is characterised as subjective
- TGA demonstrated capability for OAIC but refused for citizen requests despite 92% scope reduction

Both issues concern TGA's capacity and willingness to organise documentation by Plan framework, but address different stages of the accountability process.

## Conclusion

FOI 25-0166 demonstrates a documented contradiction in TGA's institutional position regarding capability to classify documents by COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan objectives. TGA successfully performed this classification for the OAIC in September 2024 (2,218+ pages across 531+ containers) but characterised an identical task as impracticably subjective when requested by a citizen in June 2025—despite the applicant achieving a 92% scope reduction (399 documents to 30 documents) in direct response to TGA's consultation guidance.

The contradiction is particularly significant given TGA's initial indication (24 April 2025) that refined items "may be manageable," followed by refusal (3 June 2025) on subjectivity grounds after the applicant narrowed scope to 30 documents across 12 explicitly defined categories. This progression raises questions about whether the consultation process was conducted in good faith or whether the practical refusal outcome was predetermined regardless of scope refinement.

This contradiction, combined with the search methodology inadequacy documented in OAIC MR22/00538, raises questions about whether TGA maintains implementation records in a manner that enables verification of Plan delivery, or whether institutional resistance to transparency prevents access to records that may exist.

The OAIC FOI Guidelines (paragraph 3.68) note that a practical refusal reason may be unreasonable if the work involved has already been undertaken by the agency for other purposes. TGA's September 2024 classification of 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives for OAIC review establishes prima facie evidence that classifying 30 documents by identical criteria nine months later does not constitute an unreasonable diversion of resources—particularly when the applicant responded to TGA guidance by achieving a 92% scope reduction.

The question of whether practical refusal provisions under s24(1)(b) can legitimately be applied to classification tasks TGA demonstrably performed for oversight bodies nine months earlier—and initially characterised as "may be manageable"—remains for the OAIC or reviewing tribunals to determine. The documented capability to classify 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives for OAIC review provides a benchmark against which the "unreasonable diversion" claim for 30 documents can be assessed.
