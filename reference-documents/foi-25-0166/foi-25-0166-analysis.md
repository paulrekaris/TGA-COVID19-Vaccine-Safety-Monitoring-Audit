# FOI 25-0166: Practical Refusal and Document Classification Contradiction

**Request Date:** April 2025  
**Decision Date:** 3 June 2025  
**Outcome:** Practical refusal under s24(1)(b) FOI Act 1982

-----

## Summary

FOI request 25-0166 sought implementation documentation for the TGA COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan across five Plan objectives. After two rounds of consultation and scope refinement, TGA refused the request under practical refusal provisions, claiming that classifying documents by Plan objectives required “subjective interpretation” despite having successfully classified 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives for the OAIC nine months earlier (September 2024, MR22/00538).

-----

## Request Scope

### Initial Request (April 2025)

Broad request for implementation documentation across TGA’s COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan objectives, estimated to cover approximately 399 documents.

### Five Plan Objectives Referenced

1. Report collection
1. Signal detection and investigation
1. Action on concerns
1. Public communications
1. Stakeholder collaboration

### Requested Document Categories (12 categories proposed 20 May 2025)

1. Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) or equivalents
1. Case processing instructions/guidelines
1. Coding/process updates
1. Workflow process documentation
1. Safety signal investigation protocols
1. Regulatory action processes
1. Public COVID-19 safety communications procedures
1. Stakeholder collaboration documentation
1. Official procedure documentation
1. Internal policy/guidance
1. Implementation guidelines
1. Operational protocols

### Refined Scope (20 May 2025)

Six most recent documents per category for each of the five Plan objectives, covering the period February 2021 to February 2025. Total requested: 30 documents.

Fallback provision: Other TGA-classified documents if fewer than six available per category/objective. The reference to “TGA-classified” documents implies TGA maintains classification systems that could identify documents by category and objective.

## TGA’s Refusal Rationale

### Stated Grounds

TGA refused the refined request (Item 2) on grounds that:

1. Classifying documents by Plan objectives required “subjective interpretation”
1. Linking documents to specific Plan objectives involved judgment about “most significant” relevance
1. The task was impracticable despite scope refinement to 30 documents across 12 defined categories

### Practical Refusal Provisions Applied

Section 24(1)(b) of the FOI Act 1982: Request would substantially and unreasonably divert agency resources.

### Section 24AA(2) Requirements

Section 24AA(2) of the FOI Act requires agencies to assess whether a request would substantially and unreasonably divert resources without regard to the reasons for making the request or the applicant’s characteristics.

However, TGA’s prior demonstrated capability to classify 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives for OAIC review (September 2024) raises questions about whether classifying 30 documents across 12 predefined categories constitutes an “unreasonable” diversion of resources, particularly when the classification methodology had been successfully applied nine months earlier.

## Observations

### Demonstrated Classification Capability

TGA’s position on capability to classify documents by Plan objectives changed between September 2024 and June 2025:

**September 2024 (OAIC MR22/00538):**

- Successfully organised and classified 2,218+ pages of vaccine safety documentation
- Searched 531+ TRIM containers systematically
- Classified material by Plan objectives for Information Commissioner review
- Demonstrated technical and methodological capacity for objective-based classification

**Source:** Therapeutic Goods Administration. (2024). *Response to written notice and direction under ss 55V(2) and 55(2)(e)(ii) of the Freedom of Information Act 1982*. 20 September 2024. OAIC Reference MR22/00538, pp. 28-32.

**June 2025 (FOI 25-0166):**

- Identified scope covering approximately 399 documents
- Characterised classification by Plan objectives as requiring “subjective interpretation”
- Refused to process request despite applicant refining scope to 30 documents across 12 defined categories
- Applied practical refusal provisions nine months after demonstrating identical classification capability

### Contradiction in Institutional Position

The refusal demonstrates contradictory institutional positions:

1. **Capability demonstrated:** TGA organised 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives when directed by OAIC (September 2024)
1. **Capability denied:** TGA characterised organising 30 documents by Plan objectives as impracticably subjective (June 2025)
1. **Scope differential:** OAIC-directed search covered 531+ containers; refined FOI request sought 30 documents across 12 predefined categories

The practical refusal occurred despite:

- Applicant conducting two rounds of scope refinement
- Request narrowed from ~399 documents to 30 documents
- Document categories explicitly defined (12 categories)
- Temporal scope limited (February 2021-February 2025)
- Fallback provisions for incomplete categories included

### Implications for Document Organisation

TGA’s refusal on subjectivity grounds raises questions about:

1. **Records management:** Whether TGA’s document management systems organise material by Plan objectives or activity type
1. **Institutional memory:** Whether TGA maintains systematic implementation records that could be retrieved by objective/output
1. **Verification capability:** Whether TGA can demonstrate Plan implementation if classification by Plan objectives is characterised as subjective
1. **Differential treatment:** Whether classification capability is applied differently for oversight bodies versus citizens

## Relationship to Search Methodology Issues

This practical refusal is distinct from, but related to, search methodology inadequacy documented in OAIC MR22/00538:

**Search methodology issue (MR22/00538):**

- What search terms TGA employed in TRIM searches
- Whether search strategy was adequate to locate implementation evidence
- TGA used 8 generic document-title terms, missing output-specific terminology

**Classification refusal issue (FOI 25-0166):**

- Whether TGA will organise identified documents by Plan objectives
- Whether such classification is characterised as subjective
- TGA demonstrated capability for OAIC but refused for citizen requests

Both issues concern TGA’s capacity and willingness to organise documentation by Plan framework, but address different stages of the accountability process.

## Conclusion

FOI 25-0166 demonstrates a documented contradiction in TGA’s institutional position regarding capability to classify documents by COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan objectives. TGA successfully performed this classification for the OAIC in September 2024 (2,218+ pages across 531+ containers) but characterised an identical task as impracticably subjective when requested by a citizen in June 2025 (30 documents across 12 defined categories).

This contradiction, combined with the search methodology inadequacy documented in OAIC MR22/00538, raises questions about whether TGA maintains implementation records in a manner that enables verification of Plan delivery, or whether institutional resistance to transparency prevents access to records that may exist.

The OAIC FOI Guidelines (paragraph 3.68) note that a practical refusal reason may be unreasonable if the work involved has already been undertaken by the agency for other purposes. TGA’s September 2024 classification of 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives for OAIC review establishes prima facie evidence that classifying 30 documents by identical criteria nine months later does not constitute an unreasonable diversion of resources.

The question of whether practical refusal provisions under s24(1)(b) can legitimately be applied to classification tasks TGA demonstrably performed for oversight bodies nine months earlier remains for the OAIC or reviewing tribunals to determine. The documented capability to classify 2,218+ pages by Plan objectives for OAIC review provides a benchmark against which the “unreasonable diversion” claim for 30 documents can be assessed.
