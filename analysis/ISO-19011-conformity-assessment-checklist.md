ISO 19011 is a guideline for conducting audits; it is not a certifiable standard, and this document does not certify the TGA to any standard. Where the TGA's own Plan is assessed for whether it could be tracked and verified, that assessment is made against the obligations that actually bound the TGA (its published commitments and Commonwealth records and performance-reporting law), not against ISO 19011 itself. 

ISO 19011:2018 was the edition in force throughout the period under audit and while the audit was conducted. It was superseded by ISO 19011:2026 on 27 May 2026; the 2018 edition is retained here as the standard applicable to the activity assessed. The 2026 edition is a technical revision retaining the structure and principles of the 2018 edition.

## Executive Summary

Independent audit of the TGA's COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan implementation, conducted October 2025 to January 2026 using ISO 19011:2018 as the audit method, with ANAO performance-audit methodology and Audit Insights, and open-source (OSINT) collection practice.

**Overall conclusion:** Does not conform. Seven  non-conformities are recorded.

**Conformity status:** 3 of 19 audited outputs (16 per cent) are fully documented; 16 outputs (84 per cent) are partially documented or not documented.

**The seven major non-conformities:**

1. No systematic tracking mechanism. The TGA states monitoring was never systematically tracked against the Plan.
2. Coordination protocols between surveillance systems not documented.
3. Data-linkage capability gap identified by experts in 2020 not demonstrably addressed.
4. Contradictory FOI positions on document existence across four years.
5. AusVaxSafety signal pathways to regulatory action not evidenced.
6. Public messaging ("no different") cannot be reconciled with the formal commitment to "enhanced" monitoring.
7. Expert warnings (2020) not demonstrably addressed.

**Evidence base.** The TGA's own OAIC-directed searches (531+ TRIM containers, zero implementation records); Senate evidence given under parliamentary privilege ("day-to-day processes"); Senate Question on Notice 559 (SQ25-001584); OAIC Decision [2025] AICmr 54; and systematic review of the public FOI and surveillance-report record.

**Key supporting evidence.**

- FOI 4029-03: the TGA presented Strategy 2.3 methodology to the Advisory Committee on Vaccines (September 2021), while concurrent public reports carried no such output.
- FOI 5275: the TGA does not hold the incidence data Strategy 2.3 required, indicating the AIR-denominator calculation was not performed.
- FOI 4029-05: VSIG was operational during the relevant period; the documentary record does not show the child deaths reported in that period referred to it for expert review, against the TGA's own Work Instruction threshold (FOI 4029-06).
- Surveillance analysis: the sampled reports contained no Plan-framework reference, with observable change from enhanced to routine reporting and gaps against ICH E2E and CIOMS Working Group VIII practice.
- Systematic FOI disclosure log review: a complete review of 500+ FOI releases (July 2020 – December 2025) was conducted on **10 January 2026 (4.5 hours systematic review)** , confirming the repository contains all available Plan implementation evidence; no additional records were missed.
- TGA Performance Reports 2022-23 and 2023-24 contain no explicit references to the COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan.

**Recommended action.** A corrective-action programme covering systematic tracking, documentation standards, system integration, organisational learning, and transparency.

> This is not an ISO certification audit. It uses ISO 19011:2018 as a professional method to assess conformity between the TGA's stated commitments and the documentary evidence of implementation. It does not certify the TGA to any ISO standard.

## Purpose of This Audit

This document applies ISO 19011:2018 to assess the implementation of the TGA's COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan (February 2021). It compares the TGA's stated commitments (audit criteria) against the documentary evidence of implementation (audit evidence), using an established audit method.

**Audit type (ISO 19011:2018, Introduction, Table 1).** This is a second-party "other external interested party" audit: an audit conducted by an external interested party (a private citizen and recipient of provisionally approved medicines in the relevant jurisdiction), rather than by the organisation itself (first party) or by a certification body (third party). It carries none of the access, compulsion or appointment of a statutory or regulatory audit, and it invites a formal performance audit by the ANAO with full internal access.

**Intended audiences.** Parliamentary (structure for questions and oversight); academic (peer review using a recognised method); legal (evidence quality and audit trail); professional and international (a method that can be compared across jurisdictions).

**Scope decision and why it is conservative.** ISO 19011:2018, 5.5.2, requires audit criteria to be defined so that evidence-based assessment is possible. The Plan does not number its outputs and conflates strategies with deliverables, so its criteria were not readily identifiable. The audit therefore treated each of the Plan's 17 numbered strategies as a single auditable output at strategy level, plus two governance outputs (GOV.1 implementation oversight; GOV.2 performance measurement), giving 19 outputs in total. This 19-output frame was fixed before assessment and applied uniformly to every strategy; it was not derived from the result. Disaggregating the strategies into their implied deliverables would yield about 36 outputs; the audit did not do so, in order to minimise interpretive risk and present the most conservative scope (GAO, Government Auditing Standards 2018, para. 8.10: scope is the boundary of the audit and is tied to the audit objectives; ANAO performance-audit methodology to the same effect). That the Plan was not structured to support tracking is assessed below against the Commonwealth's own performance and records obligations, not against ISO 19011.

**Out of scope.** Clinical safety or efficacy of any vaccine; the correctness of any individual regulatory decision; and individual compensation outcomes. Where compensation or claimant material appears, it is contextual only: it illustrates a downstream consequence of the documentation gap and forms no part of any finding.

## Introduction

This audit compares the TGA's stated commitments against the documentary evidence of implementation.

### Epistemological Framework and Reasoning

The audit uses two forms of reasoning.

**Deductive.** What should be verifiable if enhanced monitoring was systematically implemented: documented protocols, governance frameworks, audit trails, integration records between surveillance systems, and performance measurement against Plan outputs.

**Abductive.** When complete evidence is not accessible through FOI, what best explains the available pattern: FOI documentation gaps, OAIC findings on search adequacy, Senate evidence, bilateral-agreement requirements, and independent expert assessments.

**The central distinction.** The finding is absence of verifiable evidence, not evidence of absence. This audit cannot prove enhanced monitoring did not occur inside the TGA. It demonstrates that such monitoring cannot be verified through available Commonwealth records, and that verification gap is the accountability problem under Australia's public-sector governance framework.

**This investigation follows scientific principles of falsifiability and replicability.** The complete methodology is fully documented and publicly available, allowing independent scrutiny and verification of findings.

### Evidence Weight by Source Origin

Evidence is weighted by origin, prioritising auditee-generated and independently validated evidence over the auditor's own analysis. This weighting is distinct from the four-tier source-reliability hierarchy (Section 9.4 of the audit report, dataset codebook, and README), which ranks sources by type; a single source may carry both an origin weight and a reliability tier.

| Source origin | Evidence type | Weight |
|------|---------------|---------|
| Auditee's own systematic searches | TGA's OAIC-directed searches | Highest |
| Auditee's statements to Parliament | Senate evidence under privilege; QON 559 | High |
| Formal government records | FOI decisions under the FOI Act 1982 | High |
| Public documentation | Safety reports, performance reports | Supporting |

**Auditee's own systematic searches (highest weight).** September 2024 OAIC-directed searches, conducted by TGA officers under OAIC direction using the TGA's own classification framework. If implementation documentation existed, these searches would be expected to locate it. Result: no Plan-implementation records.

**Auditee's statements to Parliament (under parliamentary privilege).** Senate Community Affairs Legislation Committee evidence, 9 October 2025, characterising monitoring as "day-to-day processes" never systematically tracked against the Plan; and Senate QON 559 (SQ25-001584), the TGA's formal written answer, which describes activity but produces no documentary evidence of systematic implementation. Senate estimates witnesses are generally not sworn; this evidence is protected by parliamentary privilege, and knowingly giving false or misleading evidence to a committee may be dealt with as contempt. It is not characterised as given under oath.

**Formal government records (FOI decisions).** 2022 to 2025 decisions under the FOI Act 1982, including the February 2022 statement that no implementation report exists, and the related OAIC determinations.

**Public documentation (contextual).** 150+ safety reports, regulatory decisions, and TGA performance reports. This shows monitoring activity occurred; it does not document systematic implementation of the Plan's enhanced methodologies. Review of the TGA Performance Reports for 2022-23 and 2023-24 found no reference to the Plan.

Findings rely mainly on auditee-generated and formal-record evidence, with public documentation providing context.

ISO 19011:2018, Clause 6.4.7 requires that only information subject to verification should be accepted as audit evidence; where verifiability is low, the auditor must judge the degree of reliance. This weighting reflects evidential origin and independence rather than source type. Verification (cross-checking against independent sources) was achieved by cross-referencing each claim against at least one independent origin — for example, the auditee's parliamentary statements verified against formal FOI records and public reports. The combination of high-weight origin and independent verification provides the strongest evidentiary foundation.

This audit provides **reasonable assurance** (not absolute certainty) based on systematic evidence collection within defined boundaries. The finding is **absence of verifiable evidence**, not **evidence of absence**. If implementation documentation exists but was not disclosed through FOI processes (multiple requests 2022-2025), located in TGA's OAIC-directed comprehensive searches (531 containers, September 2024), referenced in Senate testimony under parliamentary privilege, or published in 150+ public safety reports across 33 months, then TGA may address audit findings by producing such documentation through appropriate channels (FOI disclosure, OAIC provision, parliamentary submission, or public release). The audit methodology is designed to be responsive to new evidence and would incorporate it with transparent version history.

**What OAIC AICmr 54 decided.** AICmr 54 found that the Department took all reasonable steps under FOI Act s 24A and that the documents cannot be found or do not exist. That supports this audit's absence finding. It is not an OAIC endorsement of the auditor's wider concerns, and it is not a finding that the TGA acted improperly: it found the FOI search process adequate. This document characterises AICmr 54 as an independent statutory finding that the implementation records cannot be located or do not exist.

**Audit context.**

- 68.4 million COVID-19 vaccine doses were administered under provisional approval (about 91 per cent of Australia's total rollout).
- Provisional approval rested on enhanced post-market surveillance and on "active and comprehensive" monitoring commitments.
- The February 2021 Plan specifies 17 numbered strategies. Two governance and performance-measurement outputs were added for assessment, reflecting standard public-administration obligations.
- National Cabinet and bilateral agreements formalised the monitoring and reporting obligations.

## Methodological Limitations

These constraints are stated openly.

**Access.** No access to TGA internal systems, databases or non-public records; no staff interviews or operational walkthroughs; all assessment is documentary.

**Evidence.** Reliant on the TGA's FOI responsiveness and record-keeping quality; limited to publicly released or FOI-obtained documents; cannot verify the completeness of the TGA's internal searches beyond the OAIC-directed process.

**Resources.** Single auditor; no formal peer review during evidence collection; a four-year window may not capture every relevant document.

**Scope.** Documentation focus only; assesses documented evidence of implementation, not internal tacit knowledge or undocumented practice; primarily Commonwealth-level, with limited visibility of state and territory implementation.

**Auditee interaction could not be obtained.** ISO 19011:2018, 6.4.8, contemplates that nonconformities are reviewed with the auditee to obtain acknowledgement that the evidence is accurate, and 6.4.3 and 6.4.10 contemplate opening and closing meetings. As a citizen audit, none of these interactive steps was available. The nearest functional substitute is used in their place: the TGA has, on the record and repeatedly, been asked whether Plan-implementation evidence exists, through four years of FOI requests, OAIC-directed searches, Senate questioning, and Senate QON 559, and has answered. Those formal answers are treated as the auditee's response to the matters raised, with the limitation that formal 6.4.8 acknowledgement of these specific findings was not obtained.

**Assurance level.** This audit provides reasonable assurance, not absolute certainty, consistent with ISO 19011, ANAO performance-audit methodology, and open-source practice. Per ISO 19011:2018, 6.4.9.1 b), conclusions are reached taking into account the uncertainty inherent in the audit process; per 6.4.10 a) and 6.5.1 k), audit evidence is based on a sample of available information, with a residual risk that the sample is not fully representative.

**If additional documentation exists.** If implementation documentation exists but was not disclosed through FOI processes (multiple requests 2022-2025), located in TGA's OAIC-directed comprehensive searches (531 containers, September 2024), referenced in Senate testimony under parliamentary privilege, or published in 150+ public safety reports across 33 months, then TGA may address audit findings by producing such documentation through appropriate channels (FOI disclosure, OAIC provision, parliamentary submission, or public release). The audit methodology is designed to be responsive to new evidence and would incorporate it with transparent version history.

**Finding statement.** The finding is absence of verifiable evidence, not evidence of absence. The audit does not claim enhanced monitoring did not occur; it finds that enhanced monitoring cannot be verified through documentation available within Commonwealth accountability frameworks.

These limitations do not materially affect findings based on the TGA's own OAIC-directed searches, Senate evidence, and independent oversight determinations.

## Audit Evidence Collection and Analysis

**Primary methods.**

1. FOI Act 1982 processes: systematic requests targeting each strategy; internal review when responses were inadequate; OAIC review for contested decisions.
2. Open-source collection: systematic collection of public TGA documents, reports and communications; analysis of Senate evidence; review of published safety reports and AusPARs; cross-referencing for contradictions; verification and source validation following recognised open-source practice.
3. ANAO audit standards: the ANAO Audit Manual, ANAO Audit Insights on performance-information assessment, and ANAO records-management and audit-evidence guidance.

**Source 1: TGA FOI responses.** 531 TRIM folders, 2,218 pages; systematic requests targeting each strategy; official documents lawfully obtained; limited by TGA control over provision and by record-keeping quality.

**Source 2: OAIC determinations.** OAIC review decisions, including MR22/00538; administrative review where FOI responses were inadequate; an independent oversight assessment.

**Source 3: TGA Senate evidence.** Evidence to Senate estimates, 9 October 2025; analysis of the published transcript; a public record given under parliamentary privilege. The TGA stated that monitoring comprised "day-to-day processes" never systematically tracked.

**Source 4: academic literature.** Peer-reviewed work on Australian pharmacovigilance systems, providing context for identified system-capability gaps.

**Source 5: public surveillance-reports analysis.** An analysis of the TGA's COVID-19 vaccine safety reports across the 33-month reporting period (March 2021 to November 2023). Key findings:

1. No Plan-framework reference in the sampled reports, and none located on wider review of the series: no reference to the Plan as an organising framework, and no Strategy 2.3 outputs.
2. Internal capacity, public opacity. FOI 4029-03 (September 2021) shows the TGA presented Strategy 2.3 methodology to the Advisory Committee on Vaccines (proportional reporting ratio analysis, MaxSPRT, AIR-denominator analysis, observed-versus-expected method), while concurrent public reports carried no Strategy 2.3 reference and no statistical-methodology disclosure.
3. Observable change in reporting over five periods, from weekly detailed signal investigations (2021) to "expected rates" language without method (2022), standardised reassurance messaging (late 2022), "no new concerns" statements (early 2023), and wind-down to cessation without an evaluation (late 2023).
4. ICH E2E practice not met. From mid-2022, reports used "expected rates" language without publishing baseline data sources, calculation methods, confidence intervals, or power calculations.
5. CIOMS Working Group VIII benchmark not met. CIOMS WG VIII (2010), Chapter VIII, sets out traceable documentation linking signal detection through assessment to regulatory decision, including a documented rationale where signals do not lead to action. No signal-to-action audit trail is present in the reports.
6. OAIC search-scope gap. The September 2024 searches did not include Advisory Committee containers (where FOI 4029-03 sits) and did not use terms such as "Advisory Committee" or "ACV", forums where Plan implementation would reasonably be discussed.
7. Compensation context (contextual only, not a finding). Of 4,962 COVID-19 Vaccine Claims Scheme applications, 522 (about 10.5 per cent) were approved. This is included to illustrate a downstream consequence of inaccessible signal-investigation documentation; it forms no part of any finding.
8. Plan design and accountability. Assessed against the Commonwealth's own performance and records obligations (PGPA Act ss 37 to 39; PGPA Rule s 16F(2)) and ANAO performance-audit methodology, the Plan did not number its outputs, conflated strategies with deliverables, and included no implementation framework, so it did not support systematic tracking of the TGA's commitments from the outset. This is a shortfall against the Commonwealth performance and records obligations applying to the Department, not against ISO 19011, which is the auditor's method.

Extensive monitoring activity occurred (140,000+ DAEN reports, millions of AusVaxSafety responses, the investigated signals, 57 actions, 150+ reports). The absence of Plan-framework references and statistical-methodology disclosure in the public reports indicates the Plan did not function as a public accountability framework.

### Sampling Method and Its Robustness

Where this audit reviewed the full FOI disclosure log (500+ releases) and the full public safety-report series for the existence of Plan-framework references, that is a full-corpus review and is stated as such.

Where the analysis examined the content and method of the safety reports in detail, it used judgement-based sampling (ISO 19011:2018, A.6.2) of six reports drawn from the 150+ published across the 33-month period. The sample was built on three criteria:

- Phase representation: reports from the enhanced, transition and routine phases.
- Temporal span (beginning, middle, end): reports capturing programme initiation, peak rollout and cessation.
- Frequency variation: reports from the weekly, biweekly and fortnightly cadences.

**Why the sample is robust for the question asked.** The feature tested, whether the reports were structured around or referenced the Plan and whether they disclosed signal-detection method, is a structural and editorial characteristic of the report format, not a rare or randomly distributed event. The reports are standardised in format across the series. A characteristic that forms part of a standardised report's structure either recurs across the series or it does not; it does not appear sporadically. A sample spanning the full timeline, every phase and every cadence therefore reliably detects whether the format ever incorporated the Plan framework or methodology disclosure. Had the Plan functioned as an organising framework for public reporting, it would surface structurally and repeatedly; its absence across a beginning-to-end, all-phase sample is strong evidence of systemic absence across the corpus.

**Stated limitation (A.6.2).** Judgement-based sampling permits no statistical estimate of the uncertainty in the resulting finding. The finding is therefore expressed as: no Plan-framework reference or signal-detection methodology disclosure was identified in the sampled reports, and none was located on wider review of the series. It is not expressed as a statistically quantified universal. The finding is falsifiable: production of a single report demonstrably structured around the Plan, or disclosing the Strategy 2.3 methodology, would qualify it and would be incorporated into a later version.

### Systematic FOI Disclosure Log Review

To verify that no Plan implementation evidence was missed, a systematic review of TGA's complete FOI disclosure log (500+ releases, July 2020 – December 2025) was conducted on **10 January 2026 (4.5 hours systematic review)** . Method: keyword search across the full log using Plan-aligned terms, with matches cross-referenced against the repository. Results: 25 COVID-safety FOIs identified; core documents already in the repository (FOI 4029, FOI 5082); no new high-value documents; no Plan-framework references. Three promising candidates (FOI 25-0220, FOI 2389-01, FOI 3727-04) were examined in full and contained raw AEFI data, individual case reviews, or pre-approval evaluations without Plan-framework references. The documentation gap is therefore systemic across the FOI record reviewed, not the product of selective sampling. The repository contains all available Plan implementation evidence from the complete FOI corpus.

### Comparative Search Methodology Analysis

A comparison was conducted between TGA's OAIC-directed search strategy (September 2024) and this audit's search method to validate that the evidence collection was comprehensive.

**TGA's OAIC-directed searches (MR22/00538):** 8 search terms; document title-matching approach (e.g., "Implementation of COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan", "Audit report COVID-19 vaccine safety monitoring"); 531+ TRIM containers searched; result: zero implementation records found.

**This audit's search strategy:** 14 output-specific and activity-based search terms (e.g., "signal detection", "AusVaxSafety coordination", "data integration", "enhanced monitoring", "AEFI escalation", "performance metrics"); scope: public documents, FOI releases, parliamentary records, partner publications; result: identified the same evidence gaps plus additional validation through systematic FOI corpus review.

**Key difference:** TGA searched for document titles; this audit searched for implementation activities and outputs. That difference in approach raises questions about the "all reasonable steps" standard under FOI Act s 24A.

## Audit Findings

Each finding states the criterion, the evidence, the finding and grade, and the impact. Findings are graded per ISO 19011:2018, 6.4.8, which permits qualitative grading such as major and minor. A major non-conformity denotes a systemic failure to demonstrate fulfilment of a defined requirement.

**Documentation coverage.** 3 of 19 outputs (16 per cent) have fully traceable documentation; 16 of 19 (84 per cent) are partially documented or have no identifiable implementation records.

**A note on the signal figures.** Two official TGA figures exist, and the difference between them is itself relevant. In Senate evidence (9 October 2025) the TGA stated 148 signals investigated and 57 regulatory actions taken, leaving 91 investigated signals (about 61 per cent) with no publicly documented decision rationale. In Senate QON 559 (SQ25-001584), the TGA's later written answer stated 150 signals and 57 actions, leaving 93 with no documented rationale. Both are reported as given. That the agency's own signal count differs between spoken evidence (148) and written answer (150), with no reconciliation, is consistent with Finding 1.

### Finding 1: systematic tracking mechanism (major)

**Criterion.** The Plan's 17 strategies, as the TGA's own published commitment, and PGPA Act s 38 (duty on the accountable authority of the Department to measure and assess the entity's performance).

**Evidence.** Senate evidence (9 October 2025) that monitoring was "day-to-day processes" never systematically tracked against the Plan; no documented tracking mechanism in any FOI release; no performance measurement against Plan outputs; the agency's own signal count differs between testimony (148) and written answer (150). Review of the TGA Performance Reports for 2022-23 and 2023-24 found no explicit references to the COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan.

**Finding.** Major non-conformity. No systematic mechanism existed to track implementation of the Plan.

TGA’s formal OAIC submission (20 September 2024, MR22/00538, paragraph 15) states: “there is unlikely to be a set of discrete documents evidencing the TGA’s preparedness (because no audit of preparedness was undertaken).” This is the Department’s own acknowledgement in formal proceedings that no internal mechanism existed to assess whether the Plan’s outputs were achieved — directly corroborating this finding from within TGA’s own documentary record.

**Impact.** Conformity with the formal monitoring commitment cannot be demonstrated; there is no accountability framework against which delivery could be assessed.

### Finding 2: coordination protocols (major)

**Criterion.** The Plan's positioning of AusVaxSafety and the integration of surveillance systems (Strategies 2.1, 2.4).

**Evidence.** OAIC matter MR22/00538: no coordination protocols produced; a peer-reviewed assessment describing "parallel systems" where notifications may "fall through gaps"; no documented integration or data-sharing mechanism in any FOI release.

**Finding.** Major non-conformity. No objective evidence of documented coordination between the major surveillance systems.

**Impact.** Comprehensive safety assessment cannot be shown to have been systematically enabled.

### Finding 3: data-linkage capability (major)

**Criterion.** ICH E2E practice on data linkage for systematic signal detection (recognised benchmark).

**Evidence.** Pre-rollout expert assessment (Phillips et al., interviews 2020; published 2021) describing data linkage as a major gap though technically feasible; Senate evidence (2025) consistent with the gap persisting; no evidence of data-linkage infrastructure developed in the audit period.

**Finding.** Major non-conformity. An expert-identified gap in data-linkage capability was not demonstrably addressed over a multi-year opportunity.

**Impact.** Reduced documented capacity for population-level signal detection.

### Finding 4: contradictory FOI positions (major)

**Criterion.** FOI Act 1982 s 24A (reasonable-steps and document-existence) and the consistency expected of formal government records. ISO 19011 has no "transparency principle"; the criterion is the statutory and records standard, not the audit guideline.

**Evidence.** Feb 2022 (FOI 3643): the requested implementation report "does not exist," yet TGA states it holds "ample documentation demonstrating compliance" with the Plan's objectives — identifying no Plan-specific implementation record. 2024 (MR22/00538): OAIC-directed searches across 531+ containers identify zero documents in scope. June 2025 (FOI 25-0166): TGA declines on practical-refusal grounds, stating that classifying documents by Plan objective would require "subjective interpretation" because they "have not been previously identified or related to that objective."

**Finding.** Major non-conformity. Materially inconsistent positions on the existence and retrievability of implementation documentation across four years.

**Impact.** Undermines the ability to assess implementation from the documentary record, and indicates documentation practices inadequate for audit purposes. The audit records the documentary inconsistency as the finding; whether it reflects absent procedures or institutional defensiveness is a question for a body with internal access, and no motive is attributed.

### Finding 5: AusVaxSafety signal pathways (major)

**Criterion.** The Plan's positioning of active surveillance as informing regulatory action; CIOMS Working Group VIII practice on traceable signal-to-action documentation (recognised benchmark).

**Evidence.** About 6.8 million AusVaxSafety responses collected; 148 to 150 signals investigated and 57 actions taken (see the note above); no documented pathway connecting active-surveillance outputs to specific signals or actions; the OAIC searches omitted "AusVaxSafety" as a search term; QON 559 does not mention AusVaxSafety in the TGA's formal answer on collaborations, despite Strategy 2.4. Peer-reviewed practice (Malikova 2020) describes signal management as including tracked, documented actions at every stage; against that benchmark, the absence of a documented pathway is a material traceability gap. CIOMS Working Group VIII (2010), Chapter VIII, requires traceable documentation linking signal detection through assessment to regulatory decision, including documented rationale for signals not resulting in action. TGA reports 148 signals investigated and 57 regulatory actions taken, and no documented decision rationale is available for the 91 signals (about 61 per cent) that did not result in a recorded regulatory action.

**Finding.** Major non-conformity. No objective evidence of a documented pathway from active surveillance to regulatory action.

**Impact.** It cannot be shown from records whether a major surveillance investment informed regulatory decisions.

### Finding 6: public communication versus policy commitments (major)

**Criterion.** Consistency between public communication and formal policy commitment.

**Evidence.** 3 February 2021 departmental video: COVID-19 vaccines "will be no different" from routine surveillance. February 2021 Plan: "enhanced" monitoring across 17 strategies. National Cabinet (November 2020): "active and comprehensive" monitoring. Bilateral agreements (February 2021): enhanced monitoring and systematic reporting. October 2025 Senate evidence: routine "day-to-day processes".

**Finding.** Major non-conformity. The public "no different" message and the formal "enhanced" commitment cannot both be operationally true. The TGA's own later characterisation aligns with "no different", leaving the "enhanced" commitment unevidenced as a distinct, tracked framework. Three interpretations are open: the public was assured of more than was operationalised; the commitments were aspirational; or the TGA could not articulate what distinguished enhanced from routine. Each points to the same documentary outcome.

**Impact.** The basis on which the public was assured of heightened oversight cannot be demonstrated from records. Between March 2021 and June 2023 the TGA published more than 150 weekly reports, then moved to monthly from July 2023; these reports provide aggregate adverse-event data but do not show how the Plan's enhanced strategies were implemented, and do not provide a signal-to-action audit trail.

### Finding 7: expert warnings not systematically addressed (major)

**Criterion.** The Plan's own review and evaluation commitments and PGPA s 38. ISO 19011 has no "continuous-improvement principle"; the criterion is the Plan's commitments and the statutory duty, not the audit guideline.

**Evidence.** Expert system-gap findings (interviews 2020; published in Vaccine, 2021) recommending significant enhancement, including data linkage, system integration and population-level surveillance; Senate evidence (2025) consistent with the same gaps persisting; no documented corrective-action programme located. Further documentary evidence: FOI 4029-05 indicates the Vaccine Safety Investigation Group (VSIG) was operational during the relevant period (it assessed a fatal case in a 21-year-old in September 2022). The TGA's own VSIG Work Instruction (FOI 4029-06) records that youth deaths "had potential to change the favourable benefit-risk balance." The documentary record does not show that child deaths reported in the same period were referred to VSIG for expert review. This points to a gap between an available expert mechanism, the TGA's own stated criteria, and the documented practice.

**Finding.** Major non-conformity. No evidence that the documented expert warnings were systematically addressed over the multi-year opportunity. The VSIG/child deaths example illustrates the gap between available expert mechanisms and systematic implementation.

**Impact.** Indicates an absence of documented organisational learning on the identified gaps.

**Tally.** 7 major non-conformities; 0 minor non-conformities.

## Burden of Proof and Accountability Framework

**Commonwealth accountability standards.** Commonwealth entities operate under statutory frameworks that require documented evidence of performance.

- **PGPA Act 2013.** Section 37 requires the accountable authority to keep records that properly record and explain the entity's performance in achieving its purposes. Section 38 requires the authority to measure and assess that performance. Section 39 requires preparation of annual performance statements. PGPA Rule s 16F(2) requires those statements to include the results of the measurement and assessment of performance. Together, these require performance to be documented so that it can be measured, assessed and reported.
- **Archives Act 1983.** Section 24 prohibits the unauthorised destruction or disposal of Commonwealth records. The positive duty to keep full and accurate records of activities is set out in the National Archives Information Management Standard.
- **FOI Act 1982.** Section 24A allows refusal where documents cannot be found or do not exist, and requires the agency to have taken all reasonable steps to locate documents. Documented evidence of activities should be locatable through reasonable searches if records management is sound.

**Burden allocation.** Under these frameworks, the burden rests with the entity to demonstrate performance through documentation, not with citizens to prove documentation does not exist. This reflects information asymmetry, record-keeping obligations, the purpose of public oversight, and the principle that activities conducted on behalf of citizens require transparency.

**Applied to this audit.** The TGA published the Plan as the operational framework for enhanced monitoring under provisional approval. The burden rests with the TGA to demonstrate systematic implementation of the specified outputs, governance tracking progress, performance measurement of delivery, and documentation enabling independent verification. The burden does not rest with this audit to prove that monitoring never occurred, that officials did not work diligently, or that undocumented processes do not exist somewhere.

**Evidence of systematic absence.** Across four years: the TGA's own OAIC-directed searches of 531+ containers found no implementation-tracking documents; the TGA's own statements (no implementation report exists, February 2022; "day-to-day processes" never systematically tracked, October 2025; Objective 5 "unable to meaningfully interpret into searchable item", September 2024); independent oversight (AICmr 54 confirming the documents cannot be found or do not exist, and the Sparke Helmore administrative-law analysis); systematic review of 150+ public reports with no Plan-framework reference; systematic review of 500+ FOI disclosure log entries with no Plan-framework references; and multiple FOI processes (FOI 3643, 5082, 25-0166, 5275) returning the same result.

**The accountability gap.** Not that monitoring did not occur (activity clearly happened), but that the Plan's enhanced framework cannot be verified through documentation accessible within Commonwealth accountability mechanisms.

**Alignment with ISO 19011.** The finding statement, that implementation cannot be verified through accessible documentation, is truthful and accurate on the available evidence (ISO 19011:2018, 4 b, fair presentation), and the conclusions are drawn from evidence made available rather than from speculation about what might exist elsewhere (6.4.9).

**How the TGA could address the findings.** TGA may address these findings by:

- **Producing documentation demonstrating systematic implementation:**
  - Progress reports tracking 17 outputs across rollout period
  - Governance committee minutes documenting Plan oversight
  - Performance metrics assessing delivery against commitments
  - Integration protocols between surveillance systems
  - Signal investigation methodologies and audit trails
- **Explaining documentation absence:**
  - Clarifying why Cabinet-endorsed policy implemented without systematic tracking
  - Addressing contradiction between internal capacity (FOI 4029-03 ACV presentation) and public documentation absence
  - Reconciling "day-to-day processes" characterisation with "enhanced monitoring" commitment
- **Implementing corrective actions:**
  - Establishing systematic performance tracking against published commitments
  - Strengthening records management to meet Archives Act obligations
  - Improving FOI processes to enable verification of policy implementation
  - Creating governance frameworks ensuring accountability for delivery

**Critical point.** This audit does not claim the TGA failed to monitor vaccine safety. The finding concerns documentation and accountability, not clinical or operational competence. The question is whether the documented activity constituted systematic implementation of the published Plan, verifiable through Commonwealth accountability mechanisms.

## Comparison to High-Reliability Domains

**A note on what this comparison can and cannot show.** This comparison sets the published safety-management expectations of aviation (ICAO Doc 9859) and nuclear power (IAEA GSR Part 2) against what could be obtained about the TGA through FOI. It compares an external, partial, document-only view of the TGA with other sectors' published ideals; it does not compare internal practice with internal practice. It therefore shows that the TGA's documented and disclosable safety-management trail falls short of what those sectors publish as expected. It does not, and cannot, establish what the TGA does internally and has not disclosed.

With that stated: ICAO and IAEA frameworks treat safety management as an auditable system with documented objectives, performance indicators, continuous monitoring, and traceable decision records enabling external oversight (ICAO, 2018; IAEA, 2016). On the disclosable record, the TGA's COVID-19 monitoring lacks comparable documented performance objectives, signal-to-action traceability, and periodic documented review against the Plan, a gap underlined by the Senate evidence that monitoring relied on "day-to-day processes" never systematically tracked. High-reliability theory (Weick and Sutcliffe, 2015) describes a documentation-rich, anticipatory posture; the disclosable record here is closer to a documentation-light one, though the asymmetry caveat above applies throughout.

In healthcare, high-reliability and patient-safety frameworks developed by bodies such as WHO, the Institute for Healthcare Improvement (IHI) and the Joint Commission similarly emphasise leadership for safety, a culture that encourages reporting and learning from harm, and the routine use of data systems to measure safety performance over time. The Manchester Patient Safety Assessment Framework (MaPSAF), provides a recognised tool for evaluating safety culture in healthcare settings. TGA's limited evidence of systematic learning from expert warnings, absence of a documented signal-to-action trail, and lack of performance measures for its Safety Plan contrast with these healthcare safety expectations for data-driven learning cultures.

**The regulator's own standard for industry.** The TGA requires sponsors to maintain documented quality systems, audit trails and performance tracking under GMP, GCP and ICH E2E-compliant pharmacovigilance, as conditions of approval. On the disclosable record, the TGA's own COVID-19 monitoring does not demonstrate the same documentation discipline it requires of industry. That asymmetry is the accountability point.

## Appendix A: Direct TGA Responses

Between January and February 2022, direct email inquiries and a formal FOI request (FOI 3643) sought an implementation report on the Plan's five objectives.

Response pattern: pre-FOI emails (31 January, February 2022) gave generic assurances and four identical links to routine pharmacovigilance resources, with no Plan-specific implementation evidence; the FOI decision (27 February 2022) refused access under s 24A (document does not exist), with the explicit statement that no implementation report exists, while also referring to "ample documentation".

Significance: this statutory position is highest-weight evidence (auditee's own statutory position). The pattern of substituting routine pharmacovigilance outputs for Plan-specific implementation evidence supports non-conformity findings across several areas.

## Appendix B: OAIC-Directed Search Results (September 2024)

Context: following the February 2022 refusal, the TGA conducted an OAIC-directed search 2.5 years later (MR22/00538).

Method: two independent searchers (a senior medical officer and an assistant director, vaccines surveillance); eight search terms across the TRIM system; 531+ containers reviewed; searches included "Implementation of COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan", "Audit report COVID-19 vaccine safety monitoring", and "Implementation vaccine safety".

Results: no implementation reports; no evaluation documents; no systematic Plan-tracking documentation. The only documents located were emails to the applicant stating that no implementation report exists.

Attachment A, page 29 of the TGA’s OAIC submission (20 September 2024, MR22/00538, Attachment D: contemporaneous TRIM search records) contains the search records compiled by TGA officers during the OAIC-directed TRIM searches. One search officer recorded in real time: “I have been unable to find any relevant documents in this search. If you have any other suggestions for search terms, I will conduct further searches as necessary.” This is not legal argument or institutional response — it is an unguarded operational statement from someone actually conducting the search, expressing genuine uncertainty about where to look. Contemporaneous operational records of this kind are among the most valuable documents in a forensic evidentiary record because they capture the searcher’s candid position at the moment of the search, before any institutional position is formed.

Notably, the search officer did not limit the search to the original terms. Rows 7 and 8 of Attachment A show the officer independently added “Implementation COVID-19 vaccine pharmacovigilance plan” and “Audit report COVID-19 vaccine pharmacovigilance plan” as additional search terms — expanding the scope beyond what was requested. Both returned zero results. The inability to locate records was not the product of a constrained or narrow search design. It reflects an expanded, good-faith search that still returned nothing.

The Principal Medical Advisor’s covering email estimated the search exercise would take “no longer than 30 minutes.” (TGA OAIC Submission, 20 September 2024, MR22/00538, p. 31)

Gaps identified:

1. **AusVaxSafety omission.** The TGA acknowledged the Strategy 2.4 commitment to collaborate with AusVaxSafety but did not use "AusVaxSafety" as a search term. No documented bridge was found between the survey responses and the signals and actions.
2. **Objective 5 "unable to meaningfully interpret".** The TGA stated the collaboration commitment could not be meaningfully interpreted into a searchable item, indicating an absence of searchable records for stakeholder coordination.
3. **Search covered document types.** The TGA searched by document type (SOPs, protocols, guidelines, workflows) and located records of those types, yet found no Plan-implementation documentation — so the absence reflects non-existence, not a search that was too narrow to reach it.

Summary: 2,218+ pages of operational documents were found (AEFI processing, signal-detection procedures); no Plan-implementation tracking documents were found.

## Appendix C: COVID-19 Vaccine Doses Under Provisional Approval

Scale: 68.4 million doses administered by 23 July 2023; about 75 million total doses since February 2021; about 91 per cent administered under provisional approval.

Timeline:

- Pfizer Comirnaty: provisional 25 January 2021; full registration 13 July 2023.
- Moderna Spikevax: provisional 9 August 2021; full registration 21 April 2023.
- AstraZeneca Vaxzevria: provisional 15 February 2021; withdrawn April 2024.

Significance: enhanced post-market safety monitoring was the compensating safeguard on which provisional approval proceeded, operationalised in the February 2021 Plan. The scale of deployment heightened the materiality of documented Plan implementation as accountability evidence.

Sources: TGA COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Reports (29 June 2023, 27 July 2023); Department of Health AIR data (10 October 2024); TGA COVID-19 vaccines regulatory status history.
