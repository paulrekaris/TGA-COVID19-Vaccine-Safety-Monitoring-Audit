# Project Metadata

## Title

**Documentation Gap Analysis: Independent Audit of TGA COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan**

## Author

Paul Rekaris  
Independent researcher and public policy analyst

## Description

This independent documentary audit evaluates implementation of the Therapeutic Goods Administration's COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan (February 2021). The Plan operationalised the National Cabinet-endorsed commitment to enhanced COVID-19 vaccine safety monitoring accompanying Australia's provisional-approval framework.

The audit examines whether implementation of the enhanced monitoring commitments can be independently verified through the documentary record. It assesses 19 audited outputs comprising 17 Plan-level operational outputs and two governance outputs, using evidence assembled through Freedom of Information requests, Office of the Australian Information Commissioner review proceedings, Senate testimony and parliamentary records, systematic review of TGA publications, and open-source documentary research.

The audit covers a vaccination program in which 68.4 million COVID-19 vaccine doses — 91.2% of Australia's rollout — were administered under provisional approval accompanied by commitments to enhanced safety monitoring.

## Objective

To determine whether implementation of the enhanced COVID-19 vaccine safety monitoring commitments set out in the TGA's February 2021 COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan can be independently verified through the documentary record.

The audit distinguishes evidence that pharmacovigilance activity occurred from evidence demonstrating systematic implementation of the specific enhanced-monitoring framework described in the Plan.

## Scope

The audit evaluates documentary evidence of implementation against 19 audited outputs: the Plan's 17 numbered strategy items and their associated outputs, assessed at strategy-item level, together with two governance outputs addressing implementation oversight and performance measurement.

The audit does not assess the clinical safety or efficacy of COVID-19 vaccines, individual causality assessments, the quality of individual regulatory decisions, or sponsor compliance with individual conditions of registration.

The enhanced-monitoring commitments assessed by the audit are policy and operational commitments associated with Australia's COVID-19 vaccine provisional-approval framework. They are distinct from statutory and sponsor-specific conditions of registration under the *Therapeutic Goods Act 1989*.

## Dataset

The supporting dataset was compiled between 2022 and 2026 to support independent documentary analysis of the Therapeutic Goods Administration's COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan (February 2021).

The dataset was assembled through Freedom of Information requests, Office of the Australian Information Commissioner review proceedings, Senate testimony analysis, systematic review of Therapeutic Goods Administration publications, and open-source documentary research.

It comprises FOI decision letters, released government documents, OAIC submissions and determinations, Senate Hansard extracts, policy and surveillance documents, and an output-level evidence matrix mapping 19 audited outputs to documentary sources using a four-tier evidence classification framework.

Each audited output is assigned a documentation status under the audit framework. The evidence matrix provides the underlying classification data from which the audit's aggregate implementation and documentation findings are derived.

All summary statistics derived from the matrix — including documentation-status distribution, status-by-objective cross-tabulation and severity-rating aggregation — are directly reproducible from the underlying classification data using standard analytical tools.

The dataset and evidence matrix underpin the independent documentary audit of the TGA COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan and enable systematic assessment of implementation and documentary verifiability across the 19 audited outputs.

## Methodology

The audit applies ISO 19011:2018 auditing principles, Australian National Audit Office guidance, and open-source intelligence and documentary-analysis methods to public and FOI-released records.

Published regulatory and policy commitments were converted into predefined auditable units and assessed against documentary evidence obtained through multiple public-accountability mechanisms.

Evidence was evaluated through source identification, provenance assessment, cross-source verification, classification and reproducible output-level mapping.

Relevant international pharmacovigilance standards and recognised good practice, including ICH E2E and CIOMS guidance, provide additional analytical context.

The methodology distinguishes between evidence of related pharmacovigilance activity and documentary evidence demonstrating implementation of the specific enhanced-monitoring commitments contained in the February 2021 Plan.

## Key Findings

The audit finds a systematic failure to demonstrate implementation of the enhanced monitoring framework set out in the February 2021 Safety Monitoring Plan.

Only 3 of 19 audited outputs (16%) are fully documented. The remaining 16 outputs (84%) are partially implemented or not documented.

Across four years of investigation, the audit did not identify documentary evidence demonstrating systematic implementation of the enhanced monitoring framework against the Plan's specified commitments.

Extensive evidence demonstrates that pharmacovigilance and vaccine safety monitoring activity occurred. The documentary gap concerns whether that activity can be linked to, and demonstrates implementation of, the specific enhanced-monitoring commitments contained in the Plan.

The audit therefore distinguishes evidence that monitoring occurred from evidence that the published enhanced-monitoring framework was systematically implemented, tracked and documented.

## Institutional Findings

The documentary record identifies significant institutional findings concerning implementation, traceability, records management and regulatory accountability.

In September 2024, OAIC-directed TGA searches surfaced more than 2,218 pages of potentially relevant material across more than 531 records containers. None was identified as documentation demonstrating implementation of the February 2021 Safety Monitoring Plan against its specified commitments.

The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner subsequently affirmed the TGA's FOI decision under section 24A of the *Freedom of Information Act 1982*, finding that the requested implementation records "cannot be found or do not exist": *Paul Rekaris and Department of Health and Aged Care (Freedom of information) [2025] AICmr 54*.

Senior TGA officials told the Senate Community Affairs Legislation Committee on 9 October 2025 that vaccine safety monitoring occurred through "day-to-day processes". The available documentary record does not demonstrate systematic tracking of those processes against the objectives and outputs of the February 2021 Plan.

In its subsequent written response to Senate Question on Notice 559 (DHDA SQ25-001584), the TGA reported that 150 safety signals had been investigated and 57 regulatory and consumer-information actions undertaken. The available record does not provide a signal-by-signal audit trail linking individual signal assessments to corresponding regulatory actions or other dispositions, leaving 93 investigated signals without a publicly documented disposition in that record.

These findings do not establish that pharmacovigilance activity did not occur, that the 93 signals required regulatory action, or that individual safety signals were improperly assessed. They identify a documentary accountability problem: extensive regulatory activity is evident, but systematic implementation and tracking against the published enhanced-monitoring framework cannot be independently reconstructed from the available record.

The institutional evidence therefore raises a broader governance question: whether a nationally significant commitment to enhance an existing regulatory function was supported by implementation architecture capable of distinguishing, tracking, measuring and demonstrating the enhancement over routine pharmacovigilance activity.

## Reproducibility

The audit is designed to be independently testable and falsifiable.

The output-level evidence matrix links each audited output to its underlying documentary sources and classification basis. Summary statistics derived from the matrix — including documentation-status distributions, status-by-objective cross-tabulations and severity-rating aggregation — are directly reproducible from the underlying classification data using standard analytical tools.

An output classified as not documented can be reassessed if relevant documentary evidence is subsequently identified.

The evidence base, methodology, classifications and replication resources are openly available for independent scrutiny and reanalysis.

## Research Reuse

The dataset provides a structured evidence base for examining documentation associated with the TGA's COVID-19 vaccine pharmacovigilance strategies and Australia's enhanced vaccine safety monitoring framework.

It supports reuse in pharmacovigilance research, health policy analysis, public administration, administrative law, regulatory governance and accountability, implementation assessment, freedom-of-information research, documentary accountability, public-sector performance analysis, records management, regulatory transparency and research into information asymmetry.

The dataset may also support comparative research examining whether published regulatory and public-policy commitments can be traced to identifiable implementation processes, outputs, governance arrangements and documentary records.
