Australia’s COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring: An Independent Audit

Did Australia’s drug regulator, the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA), carry out the enhanced vaccine safety monitoring it promised? A four-year documentary audit of its compliance, tested against the plan’s own commitments.

In November 2020, National Cabinet endorsed the Australian COVID-19 Vaccination Policy, which named the Therapeutic Goods Administration's COVID-19 Vaccine Pharmacovigilance Plan as the instrument for monitoring vaccine safety. The TGA published that plan in February 2021, promising enhanced vaccine safety monitoring across the rollout. This is an independent audit and freedom-of-information investigation of whether that monitoring can be shown to have happened, assessing the TGA’s compliance against the plan’s own commitments using the international auditing standard ISO 19011:2018, Australian National Audit Office guidance, and open-source analysis of public records.

The audit assesses 19 elements of the February 2021 plan, comprising 17 operational strategy outputs and 2 governance outputs, each evaluated for documentary evidence of implementation.

It examines 68.4 million doses (91.2% of Australia’s rollout) across four years. The question is narrow and verifiable: can the implementation of the vaccine safety monitoring plan, and the regulator’s compliance with it, be independently confirmed through records, as an enhanced framework relied upon for provisional approval requires?

The audit did not identify documentary evidence that the TGA systematically implemented the enhanced vaccine safety monitoring framework described in its February 2021 plan, leaving compliance unverifiable from available records. Of 19 audited plan outputs, only 3 are fully documented; the remaining 16 are partially documented or undocumented from the available material.

What the audit found

  • 01In September 2024, directed by the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, the TGA identified no implementation records for the safety monitoring plan — a position the Commissioner confirmed in Decision [2025] AICmr 54.
  • 02Senior TGA officials testified to the Senate (9 October 2025) that monitoring occurred through routine “day-to-day processes”, not as the distinct, systematically tracked framework the plan described.
  • 03In its written response to Senate Question on Notice 559, the TGA reports 150 safety signals assessed and 57 regulatory actions undertaken; however, the response does not provide traceability between individual safety signal assessments and corresponding regulatory actions, nor does it describe the disposition of the remaining 93 signals. The response provides no signal management documentation demonstrating how safety signal evaluation outcomes are linked to the reported pharmacovigilance activities or how the plan's 17 strategy outputs were monitored.
  • 04The methodology is open, falsifiable and replicable. Every finding is traceable to a public or FOI-released source, and the full evidence base is published for independent scrutiny.

Questions and answers

Each answer below is framed by the audit's evidentiary standard: what can be confirmed on the documentary record.

Did the TGA implement its COVID-19 vaccine safety monitoring plan?

The audit found that 16 of 19 plan outputs are partially documented or undocumented, and the regulator could produce no implementation records when directed to search for them.

Who conducted this research?

An independent researcher, applying the international auditing standard ISO 19011:2018 to public and freedom-of-information records. It has been cited in the Australian Senate and submitted to the Australian National Audit Office. It is an independent citizen audit and invites the formal audit its findings call for.

Can the findings be checked?

Yes. Every finding is traceable to a public or FOI-released source, and the full evidence base, methodology and dataset are openly published under a CC BY 4.0 licence for independent verification.

Can the audit be proven wrong?

Yes — and it is built to make that possible. The audit adopts a falsifiability principle: every finding is stated so that it can be tested against evidence and revised if the evidence requires. Any output classified as not documented can be overturned by the production of a single relevant record. The findings hold only while the documentary record remains absent, and any correcting evidence would be incorporated with transparent version history.
