# TGA COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Audit

> Independent documentary audit of the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration's February 2021 COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan. The project examines whether implementation of the TGA's enhanced monitoring commitments can be independently verified from the documentary record.

Title: Documentation Gap Analysis: Independent Audit of TGA COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Monitoring Plan
Author: Paul Rekaris
ORCID: https://orcid.org/0009-0000-1338-9578
Version: 1.9.5
Year: 2026
Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)

Paul Rekaris is an independent researcher and public policy analyst with
30 years' experience spanning regulatory reform, legislative interpretation,
governance, compliance and risk across government, higher education and finance.

**Scope**

The audit assesses whether implementation of the TGA's enhanced monitoring
commitments can be verified from the documentary record. It does not find
that monitoring did not occur, and does not assess vaccine safety.

The February 2021 Plan is a policy commitment, not a statutory instrument.
Obligations on vaccine sponsors under the Therapeutic Goods Act are separate
from the TGA's own Plan commitments. Record-keeping and performance duties
under the PGPA Act attach to the accountable authority of the Department,
not to the TGA or to the Plan.

**Regulatory context**

The analysis maps the relationship between the National Cabinet-endorsed
Australian COVID-19 Vaccination Policy commitment to enhanced safety
monitoring, the TGA's February 2021 Safety Monitoring Plan, the statutory
provisional-registration framework, sponsor obligations, Commonwealth
accountability requirements, and relevant international pharmacovigilance
guidance.

The analysis distinguishes the policy and operational commitments made under
the Vaccination Policy and Plan from the separate statutory and regulatory
obligations applying to sponsors, and from Commonwealth accountability
requirements applying to the Department.

The AusPARs associated with the transition of Comirnaty and Spikevax from
provisional to full registration document assessment of sponsor-specific
requirements, including pharmacovigilance, risk-management and
confirmatory-study requirements. The audit did not identify a corresponding
assessment in those records of implementation of the TGA's February 2021
Safety Monitoring Plan or its 17 numbered strategy items.

**Key finding**

Of 19 audited outputs, comprising the Plan's 17 numbered strategy items and
two additional governance outputs, 3 (16%) were assessed as Fully Documented,
10 as Partially Documented, and 6 as Not Documented.

These classifications concern the documentary evidence available to verify
implementation. They do not establish that undocumented activities did not occur.

**Research principles**

The audit is grounded in principles of public accountability and transparency:
where public institutions make significant commitments, their implementation
should be capable of independent verification through an adequate documentary
record.

The methodology applies ISO 19011:2018, Australian National Audit Office
guidance and open-source analysis of public and freedom-of-information records.
The evidence base and methodology are openly published to support independent
scrutiny, replication and correction.

The audit adopts a falsifiability principle. Findings are framed so that they
can be tested against documentary evidence and revised where contrary evidence
is produced. A classification of Not Documented is therefore not a finding
that an activity did not occur; production of sufficient relevant evidence
can change the corresponding assessment.

**Parliamentary and oversight context**

The author's FOI work was subsequently taken up in questioning of senior TGA
officials in the Australian Senate. The audit and related research findings
were subsequently cited in the Senate in March 2026.

Related documentary-access and accountability issues remain the subject of
two active oversight matters: an Information Commissioner review concerning
access to Plan-implementation records (MR25/01153) and a Commonwealth
Ombudsman complaint concerning related FOI and administrative issues
(2025-806374).

**Interpretation**

"Not Documented" means that sufficient locatable documentary evidence was
not identified to verify the relevant activity.

The audit distinguishes routine pharmacovigilance from the enhanced monitoring
commitments described in the February 2021 Plan. Evidence of routine
pharmacovigilance does not by itself establish implementation of each Plan
commitment.

The audit assesses documentation, governance and regulatory accountability.
It does not assess the clinical safety or efficacy of COVID-19 vaccines or
the appropriateness of individual regulatory decisions.

**Research status**

The audit report is an independent research output and has not itself been
formally peer reviewed. Research papers arising from the project have been
submitted for academic peer review.

All primary-source evidence, methodology and assessment materials are made
available to support independent scrutiny, replication and correction.
